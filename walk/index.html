How to walk around

  • Click the view to look around with the mouse
  • W A S D or the arrow keys to walk
  • Shift to run
  • Fly mode: hold the left mouse button to fly forward, the right (or Space) to go up, C/Q down; E still opens gates, lids and doors
  • M for the tape measure: click two points
  • In the pool, C ducks under; Space at the wall climbs out
  • Esc to stop looking and get your mouse back
  • The stick bottom left to walk
  • Drag anywhere else to look
  • Fly mode: the arrows on the right go up and down

WA pool rules: the fence must be 1.2 m high on the outside, with nothing to climb on within its 900 mm non-climbable zones unless it's 1.8 m high. The pavilion's concrete pillar sits 0.4 m outside the east fence.

North fence

Boundary fence

i

The west boundary along the pool is part of the pool barrier, so under WA's pool rules it has to be at least 1.8 m high, measured from the ground on our side, and have nothing to climb in its top 900 mm on our side (that includes the face of the fence itself). It's the dividing fence, so any change needs the neighbour to agree. Today shows the existing fence.

Along the boundary wall

i

Whatever goes here stays under 0.9 m high. The boundary fence is the pool barrier on this side, and nothing you could climb may be in its non-climbable zone (NCZ 5): the 900 mm down from its top on our side, so 0.9 m and higher above the paving. The bed's walls (0.45 m) and the pots' rims (0.55 m) are well under it and the plants are soft: no trellis, and no shrubs grown woody up there.

Lighting after dark

i

Six ways to light the yard, one at a time. Every scheme keeps the fire pit, the house's own lights, the pump room's battens, the LED strips on the lounge's step nosings and the step lights in the canal-facing terrace wall. Each needs a 12 or 24 V LED driver, conduit to the boundary wall, promenade and jetty path before the paving goes down, and switching or a scene controller to choose between them (plus an RGB controller for Colour).
